The break reminder your eyes thank you for

Yonder reminds you to look up from your screen every 20 minutes — gently, at the right moment, and never in the middle of a sentence. Free and open source for Windows, macOS and Linux.

Download Yonder Star on GitHub

Available for Windows, macOS (Homebrew) and Linux — or build from source.

Why "Yonder"?

yon·der.

/ˈyän-dər/ · adverb

Over there, at a distance. Which is exactly where your eyes should be every 20 minutes.

That's the 20-20-20 rule: every 20 minutes, look at something about 20 feet (6 m) away for 20 seconds. The name is the instruction.

Runs right here in your browser. Nothing is recorded.

More than eye breaks

Posture & blink reminders

Tiny glass widgets appear on your screen, do their thing, and get out of the way. No windows to close.

Sit tallA posture nudge that rises up — and so do you.
BlinkA friendly reminder to blink — screens make us forget.

Plus up to 5 custom reminders — start from a preset like a warm compress, eye drops or a glare check, or write your own — each with its own icon, interval and sound. They show one at a time: when two are due together, the one that waited longest goes first and the other waits its turn.

And the details

All in Settings, all optional.

Any sound you like

Fifteen soft built-in chimes and tones, your own MP3, WAV, OGG or M4A files, or complete silence.

Your own messages

Write the words you see on the break screen; Yonder rotates through them.

Skip difficulty

Casual (skip any time), Balanced (skip after a moment) or Hardcore (no skipping).

Office hours & planned breaks

Set working hours per weekday, and plan around lunch or a daily walk.

Pauses your music

Optionally pause music and video when a break starts and resume them after: only what Yonder paused. Linux needs playerctl.

Never stuck on hold

If a call or a fullscreen app keeps smart pause on, the break still comes after an hour (or the limit you choose).

Streaks & a year heatmap

This week against last, your current and best streak, every day of the past year at a glance, and export to CSV or Markdown.

Countdown in the tray icon

On Windows, see the minutes left right in the tray icon. Double-click it to open Settings.

Evening filter

Warms and dims your screen from the evening to the morning, fading in and out. Off by default; Windows, macOS and Linux on X11.

Every monitor

Each screen gets its own break with its own blurred desktop, monitors can come and go mid-break, and the heads-up follows the screen you use.

For power users

Automations

Control Yonder from the keyboard or from your scripts with yonder:// links and command-line options (yonder --pause 30, yonder://break), and hook it into the rest of your setup with a shell command that runs on every break. See every command.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Is Yonder really free?

Yes. Yonder is open source under the MIT license. There's no paid tier, no trial and no feature held back.

Will Yonder improve my eyesight?

No, and nothing that reminds you to take breaks can. Regular look-away breaks, blinking and posture checks are widely recommended for eye comfort and can ease the tired, dry-eyed feeling of long screen days. Yonder doesn't diagnose or treat anything and isn't a substitute for an eye exam; if your eyes hurt or your vision changes, see an eye doctor.

How is Yonder different from LookAway?

LookAway is a polished, paid break reminder for the Mac, and it inspired a lot of Yonder’s feel — if you’re on a Mac and happy to pay, it’s a great choice. Yonder is a free, open-source (MIT) alternative that also runs on Windows and Linux, with no account and no telemetry. LookAway has things Yonder doesn’t, like an iPhone and iPad companion. Yonder is an independent project, not affiliated with LookAway or Mystical Bits.

Which platforms does it run on?

Windows 10 and newer, macOS 10.15 and newer, and most modern Linux desktops. Installers are attached to every GitHub release, or you can build it yourself. A few features differ by platform — the docs list them.

Does Yonder collect any data?

No. There are no analytics, no crash reporters and no accounts. Settings and stats are stored locally, and nothing is sent over the network. The blurred-desktop and split-screen styles take a snapshot of your screen for the break screen; it never leaves your computer.

How does smart pause know I'm in a meeting?

It uses simple local system signals. On Windows and macOS it checks whether any app is using your microphone or camera; on Linux, whether something is actively recording from the mic via PulseAudio/PipeWire. It can also wait while Do Not Disturb is on or while you share or record your screen. Fullscreen detection works on Windows and macOS, and focus apps — matched by process name — work everywhere. Smart pause never records audio or video.

Why is it so small?

Yonder is built with Tauri: a Rust core plus your operating system's built-in webview, rather than a bundled copy of a whole browser. That keeps the download around 10 MB.

How can I support Yonder?

If it helps you, you can chip in through GitHub Sponsors. Starring the repo, reporting bugs, improving the docs and telling a friend help just as much.

How do I build it from source?

Install Rust (via rustup) and Node.js 20 or newer, plus the platform webview dependencies listed in the Tauri docs. Then:

git clone https://github.com/RMoura98/yonder
cd yonder/app
npm install
npm run tauri dev     # run in development
npm run tauri build   # produce an installer
What is the 20-20-20 rule?

A habit often recommended by eye-care professionals: every 20 minutes, look at something about 20 feet (6 metres) away for at least 20 seconds. Yonder's Balanced mode follows it out of the box.

Yonder is a wellbeing tool, not medical advice — if you have persistent eye discomfort, talk to an optometrist.
